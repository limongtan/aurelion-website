~200 AD

1900s

2001

02 · The Deeper Frame

Most tools stop at behaviour. This one reaches underneath.

Yong's Holistic Model reads a person on three levels — and names the two needs every human brings to work. This is what separates the LPI from a label-maker: it explains why people do what they do, not just what they do.

Soma

Body

The physical engine: sleep, food, movement, energy, the nervous system carrying every workday. When the body runs empty, every dial reads worse. It's why our Life-Stage Pulse Check asks about your bio-habits before we ever open your report.

Psycho

Psyche

The mind: thoughts, emotions and will — the patterns the LPI maps across five forces and 26 styles. This is where behaviour is wired, where stress signatures live, and where self-awareness pays its dividends.

Pneuma

Spirit

Meaning, values and purpose — the thermostat of the inner life. When the spirit is well, emotions spike but don't stay spiked, and intuition stays sharp. When it isn't, regulation slips and judgement dulls. Deep transformation starts here.

Need № 1 · Social-Emotional Security

"Do I belong here? Am I accepted?"

The need to feel you truly belong to the relationships you're in. Guarded hardest by the N and A forces. When it's starving: defensiveness, silence, over-compromise — and the quiet exits that blindside managements.

Need № 2 · Inner Significance

"Do I matter here? Do I add value?"

The need to feel important to others and to add real value. Chased hardest by the R and D forces. When it's starving: showboating, credit wars, steamrolling — and burnout heroics that end in collapse.

"Pay cheques rent behaviour. These two needs buy loyalty." Every Aurelion training course uses the LPI to calibrate which need is starving in which person — so development speaks to deeper motivation, and the transformation actually holds. Source: Yong, L.M.S., LPI Holistic Model — two basic human needs. LPI Certification Programme, Day 2.

05 · Two Conflicts, Five Dials

Almost every "personality clash" is one of two gaps.

The map has two axes — so there are exactly two ways people miss each other. Learn these two, and most office drama suddenly has subtitles.

Gap 1 · The Pacing Conflict — top vs bottom

The top half is introvert · thinks first · data-orientated: they process quietly, then move. The bottom half is extrovert · acts first · action-orientated: they move, then adjust. Put them in one meeting and it feels like one side is dragging and the other is reckless. Neither is true — they're just on different clocks.

In this cohort: JG & SM (think first) vs LCH & ED (act first)

Gap 2 · The Priorities Conflict — left vs right

The left half scans the room's feelings first: is everyone okay, is everyone heard? The right half scans the outcome first: what's the deadline, what's the number? One side protects the person, the other protects the result — and each thinks the other has its priorities backwards.

In this cohort: ED, FM & ZW (people first) vs LCH, JG & SM (task first)

Now zoom in one level. Underneath the two axes, every person carries five volume knobs — one per force. Nobody's setting is wrong. But when two people's knobs sit far apart on the same force, that exact gap predicts a specific, recurring argument.

The verdict: an idea factory with its own inspection line…

In plain terms: Openness is the "new ideas" force and Analytical is the "check it properly" force — and they tie as this team's highest, at 82 each. Four of six people score 80+ on Openness, so options are never scarce; and with SM at A96 and JG at A86, every option can be stress-tested in-house. Most companies have to hire one half of that equation. This team was born with both.

…but the "pull the trigger" force runs low…

Decisive is the force that ends debates: "yes, we're doing this, by Friday, and I own it." Team average: 72 — the lowest dial on the board — and only one of six clears 80. Brilliant options pile up beautifully while the final call arrives late or not at all. It doesn't feel like a problem, because the meetings stay pleasant. But three of these six LPI reports literally prescribe, in writing, "learn to make decisions a little faster." That's the map pointing at where the money leaks.

…and the warmth is carried by too few shoulders.

Relational is the people-energy force — warming clients, lighting up rooms, making people want to stay. The team average of 75 looks healthy, until you see the spread: 92 down to 50, the widest gap on the board. Two people carry nearly all the external charm (a burnout risk wearing a smile), while the quietest strong performer may never complain about anything… right up until the resignation letter. The map flags that person months before the letter arrives.

The anchor board · who holds each force

An anchor is the strongest pair of hands on each force — the person the team leans on when that force is needed most. Healthy teams spread five anchors across many people.
