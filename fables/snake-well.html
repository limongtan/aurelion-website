One snake, three hundred mice

One winter, a huge snake slipped and fell down a deep well. At the bottom lived three hundred mice.

The mice huddled against the walls, shaking. Little Grey hid at the very back, sure tonight was the end.

But the snake knew: if all three hundred rushed it at once, it would never climb out alive.

So it curled into a corner and spoke softly:

“Don’t be afraid. I’m done hunting. I’ll only take the old and the sick. The rest of you, live in peace.”

The mice looked at each other. That night, for the first time, the well was full of snoring.

After that, every few nights, one nest sat empty. Everyone else slept just fine, thinking: not me. Not tonight.

By the end of winter, the old and the sick were gone. But the snake was still hungry.

So the strongest mice started doing it themselves, dragging the small ones to the snake to buy a few more days.

But that wasn’t the worst part.

They started bringing it food, saying the well had never been so peaceful. Three hundred mice, down to one hundred, still bowing to it.

One snake can’t beat three hundred mice.

So it never fought the crowd. It just talked to them, one at a time.

That night, it leaned close to Little Grey: “Don’t worry. You’re the one I value most.”

The next morning, the first mouse to push a friend toward the snake was Little Grey.

Is there a snake like this in your life?
