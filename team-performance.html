The five LPI forces

Which one is running your meetings?

Each force has a core need. When it’s met, you get its best. When it isn’t, you get the behaviour that drives everyone else up the wall.

OLPI Force 1 of 5

Openness

“What if we did it completely differently?”

Core need: Creativity and novelty

You’ll know them when they…

  • Says “what if we did it completely differently?” right after the plan was settled.
  • Gets bored the moment a task turns routine.
  • Brings ideas that sound wild the first time you hear them.

Under pressure

  • Grows restless and impatient with other people’s ideas.
  • Chases the big picture and drops the details.

How to get their best

  • Let them try new approaches.
  • Don’t take their frank opinions as a threat.
  • Use their knack for seeing problems differently.

How they lead

Leads with vision: paints a fresh picture of the future and gives the team permission to experiment.

Their growth move

Turn each big idea into one clear next step, and build it with the team.

NLPI Force 2 of 5

Neutral

“Can we all agree before we move?”

Core need: Stability and security

You’ll know them when they…

  • Says yes in the meeting to keep the peace, then feels quietly hurt.
  • Looks fine and never tells you there’s a problem.
  • Thrown by sudden changes and vague instructions.

Under pressure

  • Avoids conflict and puts off difficult decisions.
  • Goes slower, more careful and more sentimental.

How to get their best

  • Give them time to prepare for change.
  • Be clear and detailed with instructions.
  • Thank them for their support and loyalty.

How they lead

Leads with steadiness: creates calm and trust, listens deeply and builds agreement before the team moves.

Their growth move

Say no kindly and clearly, and voice disagreement early, while it is still small.

ALPI Force 3 of 5

Analytical

“We need more data first.”

Core need: Accuracy

You’ll know them when they…

  • Takes back a task they delegated and finishes it, because sloppy work is unbearable.
  • Wants facts, laid out logically, before anything else.
  • Flattery gets you nowhere with them.

Under pressure

  • Over-analyses, worries and delays the decision.
  • Withdraws and keeps feelings to themselves.

How to get their best

  • Don’t rush them for a decision.
  • Give them enough accurate information.
  • Keep them out of the spotlight.

How they lead

Leads with standards: decides on evidence, not noise, and spots the risks everyone else missed.

Their growth move

Decide with “good enough” data and a review date, and say appreciation out loud.

RLPI Force 4 of 5

Relational

“Let me get everyone on board.”

Core need: Recognition and approval

You’ll know them when they…

  • Runs the whole meeting and talks over the quiet ones.
  • Promises with real warmth, then forgets what they agreed.
  • Wilts in slow, formal, detail-heavy meetings.

Under pressure

  • Takes criticism personally.
  • Says yes when they mean no, to avoid upsetting anyone.

How to get their best

  • Give them room to talk, and listen.
  • Recognise their work out loud.
  • Criticise the issue, never the person.

How they lead

Leads with inspiration: energises people around a shared goal and builds buy-in across groups.

Their growth move

Write it down and follow through on every promise. Honest feedback is a form of care.

DLPI Force 5 of 5

Decisive

“Let’s decide now.”

Core need: Achievement and freedom

You’ll know them when they…

  • Finds everyone else too slow.
  • Wants the bottom line first and the details never.
  • Overrides other views to get it done, and bruises people without noticing.

Under pressure

  • Gets forceful and impatient.
  • Takes bigger risks and gets careless.

How to get their best

  • Give them a goal and room to act.
  • Keep it brief and factual.
  • Let them make the call where it fits.

How they lead

Leads from the front: decides fast, keeps momentum when others stall, takes the hard challenges.

Their growth move

Pause, ask one more question, then decide. Faster alone, further together.

Sound familiar?

It isn’t a people problem. It’s a pattern.

These collisions are predictable once you can see the forces. That’s the point of mapping a team.

NAvsRD

The fast ones think the quiet ones are slow.

And the quiet ones think the fast ones are reckless. Dr Yong calls it pacing conflict. It replays in every meeting until someone names it.

Pacing conflict · Dr Leonard Yong
NRvsAD

People-first vs task-first.

One side protects the relationships, the other protects the deadline. Both think they are protecting the team. That is priority conflict.

Priority conflict · Dr Leonard Yong
N

“Smile and let you die.”

The colleague who nods along, never pushes back, and quietly disengages. You find out when it is too late to fix cheaply.

From the LPI course on low-EQ Neutral
DvsN

One goal. One person thrilled, one person scared.

A big goal with no clear steps energises a Decisive teammate and threatens a Neutral one. Same words, opposite effect.

Dr Leonard Yong on leading mixed teams
A

The manager who takes the work back.

They delegate, then quietly redo it because sloppy work is unbearable. The team stops learning, and the manager ends up carrying it all.

Analytical under pressure
ONARD

Trained in EQ. Nothing changed.

Dr Yong’s observation: many teams have done the training, but it never transferred to the workplace. Knowing your own type, and each other’s, is where it starts to stick.

    RFor the Relational in you

    Same meeting. Five different instincts.

    A cross-functional team has to decide if a new process is ready to roll out.

    Tap each force to hear what it adds. A good decision needs all five voices.

    An illustrative exercise. A real LPI report shows each person’s blend across all five.

    O
    Could we run a smaller pilot first?

    A fresh route keeps momentum while the team learns what works.

    Try it: Ask for one alternative before the meeting moves to a decision.
    N
    Whose concerns have we missed?

    People may agree in the room and still have doubts about what happens next.

    Try it: Give each function a moment to name a concern, or say they have none.
    A
    What would tell us the pilot is safe to expand?

    A clear threshold separates evidence from assumptions.

    Try it: Agree the minimum evidence and send it early enough to read.
    R
    Who needs to be brought into this?

    The people who will use the new process can help make it workable.

    Try it: Invite one person from an affected team to review the plan.
    D
    Who will make the call, and by when?

    A named owner and a deadline give the discussion a clear finish.

    Try it: Name an owner now and put the review date in the calendar.

    The working agreement

    • Send the evidence and remaining risks before the meeting.
    • Hear a concern from each affected function and agree the smallest safe pilot.
    • Name a decision owner and set a date to review the result.

    Questions

    Before you book.

    Can someone “fail” the LPI?

    No. Everyone carries all five forces, and nobody scores zero on any of them. The report shows which forces you lean on most at work, and what that looks like under pressure.

    Is it a real psychometric tool?

    The LPI was developed by Professor Dr Leonard Yong, an educational psychologist at Universiti Malaya. He introduced the model in 1999, and its technical manual was published in 2003. Dr Yong describes it as drawing on the Big Five personality factors and the four classical temperaments. You can read the technical manual from the link in the method section.

    How long does the LPI take?

    About 5 minutes. It is 50 short questions about how you see yourself at work, and each person then receives the official LPI report scoring all five forces.

    Who sees my report?

    Each participant receives their own report. Before the Lab, we agree with you how the team map will be shared, so nobody is surprised on the day.

    Is the Team Mapping Lab HRD Corp claimable?

    Yes. Your HR team applies for the HRD Corp grant before the training, and the fee is paid from your levy once it is approved. All claims are subject to HRD Corp approval.

    Do Team Build X and S.P.A.C.E. include LPI too?

    Yes. LPI is built into Team Build X, S.P.A.C.E. Summit, S.P.A.C.E. Bridge, The Leadership Heart and Steady Under Fire. The Lab is the quickest way in if you want to start with the team map alone.
